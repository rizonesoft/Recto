1. **ISO 32000-1 Section 7.10.5 (Type 4 PostScript Calculator Functions), Table 42 — `roll` operator:**
   Let's check the direction of `roll` in PostScript (PLRM 3rd Edition, p. 642, referenced by ISO 32000-1 7.10.5)!
   In PostScript:
   `a_{n-1} ... a_0 n j roll`
   performs a circular shift of the top `n` objects on the operand stack by the amount `j`.
   "Positive `j` indicates upward motion on the stack, whereas negative `j` indicates downward motion."
    Specifically, in PostScript:
   If the stack has `a b c` (with `c` on top, `a` at bottom of the 3 elements), and we execute `3 1 roll`:
   Upward motion by 1 shifts `a` to `b`'s position, `b` to `c`'s position, and the top element `c` wraps around to the bottom (`a`'s position), resulting in `c a b` (with `b` on top)!
   And `3 -1 roll` on `a b c` shifts downward by 1: `a` wraps around to the top, resulting in `b c a` (with `a` on top)!
   Now let's check what [function.cpp](file:///R:/scratch/wt-s33-r1/src/pdf/function.cpp#L1164-L1169) and the test in [pdf_interpreter_tests.cpp](file:///R:/scratch/wt-s33-r1/tests/recto-tests/pdf_interpreter_tests.cpp#L1728-L1729) do!

   ```cpp

				const int64_t c = static_cast<int64_t>(count);
				const auto by = static_cast<size_t>(((shift % c) + c) % c);
				std::rotate(stack.begin() + static_cast<std::ptrdiff_t>(top - count),
   ```

   Wait: `std::rotate(first, middle, last)` moves the range `[middle, last)` to the beginning of `[first, last)`, and `[first, middle)` to the end (i.e. the top of the stack)!
   Let's trace `std::rotate` on `stack = [2.0, 10.0, 20.0]` (`top = 3`, `count = 3`, `shift = 1`):
